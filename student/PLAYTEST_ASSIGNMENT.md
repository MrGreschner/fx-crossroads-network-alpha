# Crossroads: Realms Beyond: Playtest, investigate and improve

**Your role:** You are a game tester and designer. Your job is to collect evidence and explain how the game could improve. You are assessed on your testing and reasoning, not your kills, mastery level or time spent playing.

## Assignment A — Testing report and three improvements

1. Make a small test plan. Choose at least three areas: one combat test, one interface/navigation test and one other area of your choice. Multiplayer is optional if a partner or connection is unavailable.
2. Record the game version, device/browser, hero and mode, and whether Game Dev mode was active. Explain how you tested each area. Include working features and unsuccessful tests, not just problems.
3. Identify **three distinct things that need improvement**. These can be reproducible bugs, usability problems, balance concerns or performance issues. Explain which kind each is. A preference is not automatically a bug.
4. For each improvement, write: a clear title; exact steps; expected and observed behaviour; frequency (such as 2 failures in 3 attempts); evidence; impact on players; a proposed change; a trade-off or possible side effect; and a test that would show whether the change worked.
5. Rank the three improvements. Explain why the first is more important or urgent than the others. State what you did not test and what remains uncertain.

Repeat each issue test three times where practical. For an intermittent crash, do not repeatedly force a failing computer to crash; record the occurrence and use a safe comparison instead. Report “not reproduced” honestly.

### Example of useful feedback

Weak: “The button is bad.”

Useful: “On my phone in landscape, I missed Secondary twice in five taps while moving. I expected the tap to trigger the skill; the hero kept moving without using it. My screenshot shows the finger-sized area I tried. I propose increasing the touch target while leaving space around the aim stick. This might cover more of the world, so I would compare successful taps and screen visibility in another five-tap test.”

This is an example format, not an observation you can claim as your own.

## Suggested test areas

| Area | Example question |
| --- | --- |
| Tutorial | Does the new-skill message explain the correct input? Can you resume and use it? |
| Combat | Does the visible attack agree with where it damages? Can allies and enemies be distinguished? |
| Character Hall | Can you find a hero, understand the abilities and enter training? |
| Mobile | Can you move and use an ability simultaneously? Does rotation preserve usable controls? |
| Multiplayer | Can you join, change teams, start, respawn and play again? Record host/guest roles. |
| Profiles | With teacher permission, does a disposable profile retain expected progress after export/import? |
| Exploration | Can you follow paths, recognise interactions and understand objectives? |
| Performance | In the same area, what changes when school mode or the FPS cap changes? |

The speed-check panel measures the current screen while solo AI is paused. It is not a benchmark of a full battle or the computer’s maximum capacity. Compare similar conditions and record limitations.

## Tools in the game

- **Menu → Options → Student playtest notebook:** record tests, three improvements and reflection; copy or download your report.
- **Copy bug-report details:** inserts version/device/game context and reporting prompts. Add your own observations.
- **Diagnostics:** view the device/frame check or download crash information. A diagnostic file supplements your report; it does not replace it.
- **Training Yard:** compare attacks, healing, projectiles and movement safely.
- **Mistglass:** study enemies you have discovered.

Notebook drafts stay on this device and are separate from player profiles. Download your report before leaving, verify it in Downloads, then clear your notes. Nothing is submitted automatically: submit through the classroom method your teacher chooses. Attach screenshots separately and refer to their filenames in your report. Use a first name, initials or assigned student code only if your teacher requests it; reports do not require account passwords.

## Game Dev testing

With teacher permission, speak to Elder Ram, choose the custom-question option and enter `gameDev`; when asked, use the current passcode provided by your teacher. This is temporary testing access. It unlocks abilities/town features but does not earn progression, scores or transferable profile unlocks. Refresh or exit Game Dev mode to restore normal progression. Notebook reports can still be downloaded. Record which mode you used. Use a teacher-approved disposable **normal** profile for profile-transfer tests.

## Suggested assessment — 30 marks (teacher may adapt)

| Criterion | Marks | Evidence expected |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Test plan and methods | 6 | At least three areas, clear steps/context, repeatable methods. |
| Three improvements | 12 | Four marks each: evidence, player impact, feasible change and verification. |
| Results and limitations | 6 | Expected vs observed, trials, passed tests, honest uncertainties. |
| Priorities and communication | 6 | Reasoned ranking, clear organisation, useful screenshots/references. |

Winning, completing a difficult realm or finding a severe bug is not required for a high-quality report.
