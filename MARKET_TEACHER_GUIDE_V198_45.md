# Market and teacher quick guide — V198.45

## Start or change a business

Click a building on the map. Its premises card describes the type, customer need, inherited assets and trade-offs. **Recommended** in Products is the previous owner's starter line; you may keep it or change it. The first business gets startup support. Additional acquisitions require district reserve savings. Public play allows five owned/trading ventures; classroom access uses teacher permissions and funds.

To change the range:

1. Close the business before editing its operation.
2. Open **Products**, select a category or **All**, and search a product name. Press Enter to apply a search. Pages contain ten entries.
3. Inspect the exact ingredient amounts, required equipment, base recipe cost and demand outlook.
4. Add chosen products to the menu. Use **Equipment → Recommended** for the selected recipes' machines, or browse All for other investments.
5. Order named ingredients in **Materials**. Quantities, minimum orders, lead times, freight and quality still matter.
6. Keep working cash for opening costs and wages. Start with small preparation buffers and adjust after observing demand.
7. Open and watch the customer queue, actual sales, missed requests, waste and profit. A demand forecast does not compensate for missing stock or inadequate service.

A pie line may need an oven, specialist preparation equipment and multiple ingredients. A mixed goods shop can also sell sandwiches, produce baskets or garments if it has all the relevant resources. It cannot run hair appointments simply by buying shampoo: specialist services need their appropriate operating model.

The blank premises starts with no inherited stock or machines. Choose **Mixed goods • build item by item** to build your own range. It receives higher first-start cash because you must buy its assets. A blank site can alternatively use a specialist service model.

## Reserve, workdays and graphs

**Reserve • transfer** at the top shows the shared expansion savings. Choose an owned business and an amount to transfer. Reserve starts at zero. Transfers obey existing loan-coverage and cash rules; do not transfer away the money needed to reopen.

The workday clock belongs to the currently selected business. Its full day is three active minutes. The separate district figure counts completed openings across businesses, not a single shared calendar day. Early closures advance the recorded opening/day sequence but are not successful completed full days.

**Sales & trends** separates:

- actual named-product totals across retained reports plus the current opening;
- last-completed-day product comparisons;
- daily units sold and operating profit for recent openings;
- a seeded, simulated demand outlook that changes every three workdays.

The simulation retains 24 operating reports; this is a useful recent-history graph, not an unlimited lifetime accounting archive. Existing summary totals remain separate. Stock, queue capacity, price, promotion and market events affect actual results.

## Assign and unlock in Manage Class Pro

1. Open **Learning Academy** and assign the required module to the intended class or bin.
2. Click **Venture access & progress**, then select a student. There is also a direct button beside each student in the progress table.
3. Choose the assigned-module context. The figures below describe the same shared classroom district used across that student's Business modules.
4. Tick the businesses the student may use. Names include descriptions such as Food cart & kitchen or Dog daycare.
5. Press **Save venture access**. A refresh/conflict protects against overwriting another teacher policy change.
6. Click a checked venture or choose an owned venture to inspect it. Unacquired premises show that they have not been purchased yet.

Permissions are per student across Business modules, not separate simulated wallets per assignment. Permission does not purchase assets or grant money. Locking preserves the business and its evidence while stopping further operation. Existing maximum active-module controls remain in Learning policy; the normal pathway is one module, up to two when approved.

## Read progress and assess evidence

The side-by-side dashboard shows the last saved cash, current workday, employees, inventory cost, loan, reputation, successful days/streak, cumulative profit, spoilage, current menu, actual sales/misses and a recent daily chart. Use Refresh saved progress for the latest synced state; the timestamp tells you how current it is. It is not live screen monitoring, and these simulation reports alone do not establish achievement.

Use **Submissions** to assess frozen work against the main outcomes. The student guidance describes what to submit, where to collect it and an example of sufficient detail. No automatic mark is awarded for wealth, profit, game time or a strong simulation score.

## Student module access

Students see modules currently assigned to them through their teacher/class membership. They do not see the full teacher catalogue or an unassigned lessons/practice route. Archiving an assignment or withdrawing the relevant link/class access closes its lesson route while retaining the underlying records. Teacher preview and ordinary public creation tools remain independent.

A module has multiple outcome parts. Students write and attach evidence for each part, save drafts and then **Submit module for review • all parts**. Partial submissions can receive feedback. The new guidance does not erase earlier responses or frozen submissions. Up to twelve relevant screenshots are supported, within the existing total upload limit; source files and reports should be reused where appropriate rather than capturing every screen.
