# Student assignment guide — V198.45

One module submission contains several outcome parts. These instructions specify useful evidence for each main outcome; examples illustrate reasoning and are not answers to copy. Teachers assess the main outcomes and may confirm observed classroom evidence. General classroom routines/competencies/pathway points do not create additional program tasks.

## ENT1010 — Challenge & Opportunity

One module submission with 4 outcome parts. Save each part as you work; Submit module for review sends all current parts together. Partial submissions can receive feedback. Teacher judges the main outcomes; examples are guidance, not extra graded subpoints.

### Part 1/4: Compare two customer needs

Main outcome: 1

**What to do:** Inspect two premises using District map → click a building → Record premises inspection. Read Customer need and Trade-off. For each, write who needs help, what problem they have and which observation supports that idea. Compare cost, usefulness and risk before recommending one.

**Where:** Studio → Explore & inspect premises; District map → click a building; return to module → Studio.

**Hand in:** Your two inspection cards (captured in Studio) and two short need/observation entries. Label any imagined customer reaction as an assumption.

**Example:** At Hearth & Ladle, customers need a quick lunch. The inherited stove could make soup, but a long queue would reduce its usefulness. At Parcel & Post, delivery helps people who cannot carry purchases. I would test the lunch queue before buying another stove.

### Part 2/4: Propose three ways to meet one need

Main outcome: 2

**What to do:** Choose a need from Part 1. Write three genuinely different responses, not just three business names. Compare who each helps. Ask a classmate or teacher which response they prefer and why; record that feedback and one change you made.

**Where:** Studio → Explore & inspect premises; District map → click a building; return to module → Studio.

**Hand in:** A three-option list or annotated sketch, the feedback you received and your revised choice. Capture your comparison board if you use it.

**Example:** For faster lunches: prepacked sandwiches, a short soup menu, or advance collection. My partner preferred sandwiches because they can eat them quickly; I changed the plan to two fillings to reduce preparation.

### Part 3/4: Build your first venture concept

Main outcome: 3

**What to do:** In Studio, confirm your planned venture after inspecting two premises. Describe what you will sell, who will buy it, its price, equipment, ingredients, staff jobs, ownership choice and one risk. Explain how the parts fit together. You do not need to purchase it yet.

**Where:** Studio → Explore & inspect premises; District map → click a building; return to module → Studio.

**Hand in:** A captured confirmed venture plan plus a concise concept paragraph covering the offering, customers, resources, ownership and risk.

**Example:** I propose a sole-owner lunch shop selling cheese sandwiches. It uses the inherited prep bench and fridge, needs bread and cheese, and keeps a small stock buffer because unsold sandwiches spoil.

### Part 4/4: Recommend proceed, revise or reject

Main outcome: 4

**What to do:** Use your inspection and concept from Parts 1–3. Compare at least two ideas against your own stated criteria, such as startup cost, customer benefit and risk. Give a proceed/revise/reject decision and name missing information that could change it.

**Where:** Studio → Explore & inspect premises; District map → click a building; return to module → Studio.

**Hand in:** A small comparison table or bullet list with your criteria, evidence for each idea and a justified recommendation.

**Example:** I would revise the pie idea: demand may be good, but the oven costs more and pies spoil. A sandwich pilot uses equipment already owned. I still need to observe how many customers leave the queue.
